Is a home NAS worth considering?

A plain-language introduction to network-attached storage, backups and ownership costs.

Research guide · 4 November 2026 · Desk research, not hands-on product testing

Disclosure: This guide is informational. It does not claim personal testing. Product features, prices and availability vary; verify current details with the manufacturer or seller.

What a NAS does

A network-attached storage device is a small computer connected to a home or office network that provides shared file storage. It can centralize documents, photos and backups for multiple devices, depending on its software and configuration.

NAS is not automatically a backup

If the NAS is the only place a file exists, a drive failure, theft, fire, malware or accidental deletion can still cause data loss. A safer plan includes another copy on a separate device or an appropriate off-site backup.

Understand the total cost

Compare the enclosure price with the cost of drives, backup storage, electricity, replacement drives and any optional software or subscriptions. Some enclosures are sold without drives. Check supported drive types and capacity limits in the official compatibility information.

Check your needs first

Consider how many people will use it, how much storage is required, whether remote access is needed, network speed, noise, power consumption and your comfort maintaining software updates and backups.

Bottom line

A NAS can be useful for people who need shared local storage and are prepared to maintain it. For a small amount of personal data, a simple external drive plus a separate backup may be easier.
