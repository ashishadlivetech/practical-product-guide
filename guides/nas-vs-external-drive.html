Home NAS vs External Hard Drive: Which Storage Setup Makes Sense?

Compare network storage with a simple external drive, understand drive bays and RAID, and plan backups without confusing redundancy with backup.

Research guide · Researched 4 November 2026 · Desk research; no hands-on testing claimed

Transparency: This is an editorial buying guide based on publicly available manufacturer documentation and other cited sources. We have not physically tested these products. Product availability, regional compatibility, prices and warranty terms can change. Confirm details for your country before buying.

The real question is how you use your files

An external hard drive is a direct-attached storage device: plug it into one computer when needed. A network-attached storage system (NAS) connects to your home or office network so multiple devices can access shared storage. A NAS can support automated backups, file sharing, photo libraries and other services, but it is also a small computer that needs setup, updates and ongoing care.

When an external drive is the better choice

  • You mainly back up one computer.
  • You need an inexpensive, portable copy of files.
  • You do not need simultaneous access from several devices.
  • You prefer a simple device with minimal configuration.

One external drive is not a complete backup strategy if it stays connected to the same computer all the time. Consider a second copy stored separately or an appropriate off-site backup. Protect the drive from theft, physical damage and accidental deletion.

When a NAS becomes useful

  • Several computers or phones need access to shared files.
  • You want a central photo or media library.
  • You need scheduled backups from multiple devices.
  • You want user permissions, file versioning or remote access and are prepared to maintain them.

Two NAS examples with published specifications

Synology DiskStation DS225+

Synology's product page lists two SATA drive bays, 2 GB of DDR4 memory, one 2.5GbE and one 1GbE network port, two USB 3.2 Gen 1 ports and support for Synology Hybrid RAID, Basic, JBOD, RAID 0 and RAID 1. The listed hardware warranty is three years, with an extension option subject to regional availability and terms. Synology notes that drives are sold separately and recommends checking its drive compatibility list.

Source: Synology DS225+ official specifications.

UGREEN NASync DXP4800 Plus

UGREEN's official product listing describes a four-bay SATA system with two M.2 NVMe slots, an Intel Pentium Gold 8505 processor, 8 GB DDR5 memory, one 2.5GbE and one 10GbE network port, and support for several RAID modes. The product listing also shows a two-year warranty. This is a more expandable hardware configuration, but it may be unnecessary for someone who only needs a simple shared folder or one-computer backup.

Source: UGREEN NASync DXP4800 Plus official product listing.

Two bays or four bays?

A two-bay NAS is often enough for a household that wants shared storage and a mirrored pair of drives. A four-bay model gives more flexibility for capacity and RAID layouts, but also raises the cost of the enclosure and the drives needed to populate it. More bays do not automatically mean better protection. Check the manufacturer's supported RAID modes and calculate usable capacity for the exact drive sizes and layout.

RAID is not a backup

RAID can help a storage system continue operating after certain drive failures, depending on the RAID level. It does not protect against theft, fire, malware, accidental deletion, software mistakes or damage to the entire NAS. A separate backup is still needed. A practical plan can include the NAS, a separate external drive that is disconnected when not in use, and an encrypted off-site copy for important files.

Costs people forget

  • Hard drives or SSDs (many NAS enclosures are sold diskless).
  • Additional backup storage.
  • Electricity for a device that may run continuously.
  • Network upgrades if you want to benefit from faster Ethernet ports.
  • Time spent on updates, user permissions, monitoring and recovery tests.

Our practical takeaway

Choose an external drive for simple, low-maintenance storage. Choose a NAS when multiple devices, shared access or automated workflows justify the extra cost and administration. The Synology DS225+ is a two-bay example; the UGREEN DXP4800 Plus is a four-bay example with faster networking and more expansion options. Neither should be treated as a complete backup by itself.
