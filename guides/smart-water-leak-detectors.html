Smart Water-Leak Detectors: What to Check Before You Buy

Compare standalone alarms and connected leak sensors, understand hub requirements, and choose placements that make sense for your home.

Research guide · Researched 4 November 2026 · Desk research; no hands-on testing claimed

Transparency: This is an editorial buying guide based on publicly available manufacturer documentation and other cited sources. We have not physically tested these products. Product availability, regional compatibility, prices and warranty terms can change. Confirm details for your country before buying.

Why a small sensor can matter

A leak often begins in a place that is not watched every day: beneath a sink, beside a washing machine, near a water heater, behind a refrigerator or close to an air-conditioning unit. A water-leak detector is designed to alert you when water reaches its sensing contacts. It can shorten the time between a leak starting and someone noticing it, but it cannot prevent every plumbing failure or guarantee that damage will be avoided.

There are two broad categories. A basic local alarm sounds when it detects water. A connected sensor can also send an app notification or trigger a compatible hub or smart-home routine. Some whole-home systems go further by monitoring flow or controlling a shut-off valve; these are different products and should not be confused with a small floor sensor.

Two documented connected sensors to compare

TP-Link Tapo T300

TP-Link lists six detection probes on the top and bottom, an adjustable alarm up to 90 dB, an IP67 rating, app notifications and a requirement for a compatible Tapo hub. Its India product page notes that the sensor operates in a specified Sub-1G band and that the sensor and hub must use compatible regional frequency bands. The manufacturer's FAQ also says the package does not include a hub. This makes the total system cost and exact local hub version important checks.

Sources: Tapo T300 product specifications · Tapo T300 hub and placement FAQ.

Aqara Water Leak Sensor T1

Aqara's published specifications list a CR2032 battery, Zigbee 3.0 connectivity, dimensions of 50 × 50 × 15 mm and an operating temperature range of 0–55°C. Zigbee sensors normally need a compatible hub or coordinator to connect to an app or smart-home platform; confirm the exact Aqara hub, region and integration support before buying. The specification page is the source for the listed hardware details, not a promise that every regional hub supports every feature.

Source: Aqara Water Leak Sensor T1 specifications.

Standalone alarm or smart sensor?

  • Choose a local alarm if you mainly need a loud sound at the property and do not need remote notifications. Check battery requirements and alarm audibility.
  • Choose a connected sensor if you want phone alerts while away. Check whether a hub, subscription, internet connection or specific app account is required.
  • Consider a shut-off system if you need the water supply to close automatically. Verify plumbing compatibility, installation requirements and what happens during a power or internet outage.

Placement is as important as the device

Place sensors where water would collect first, not simply where they are easiest to see. Typical locations include under sinks, near washing machines, beside water heaters, around refrigerators with water lines and near air-conditioning condensate drains. Follow the product's installation guidance. TP-Link specifically cautions against using the Tapo T300 outdoors to detect rainfall because natural condensation can cause false alarms.

Questions to answer before checkout

  1. Does the package include a hub, or is that a separate purchase?
  2. Does the sensor use Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Sub-1G or another protocol?
  3. Are the sensor and hub the same regional frequency version?
  4. Will alerts still sound locally if the internet is down?
  5. How are low-battery warnings delivered, and what battery does it use?
  6. Can several sensors be managed from one hub, and what is the limit?
  7. Is there a subscription fee for notifications or automation?

Who should buy one?

Leak sensors are most useful where a small leak could go unnoticed for hours: vacation homes, utility rooms, under-sink cabinets, laundry areas and properties with appliances connected to water. They are less useful if the sensor is placed far from likely leaks, its alarm cannot be heard, or notifications are not configured and tested.

Our practical takeaway

For a simple alert, a local alarm may be enough. For remote alerts, compare the full connected system—not just the sensor price. The Tapo T300's hub requirement and regional Sub-1G compatibility are particularly important to verify. Aqara's T1 uses Zigbee, so hub compatibility is also part of the purchase decision. Neither small sensor should be presented as a substitute for plumbing maintenance or an automatic water shut-off system.

How to use this guide

Use the criteria above to create a shortlist for your own setup. Before purchasing, confirm the exact model number, regional version, current price, compatibility, warranty, return policy and any required accessories with the manufacturer or seller.
