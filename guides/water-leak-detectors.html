How to choose a smart water-leak detector

A buying guide to sensor placement, connectivity, alerts and practical limitations.

Research guide · 4 November 2026 · Desk research, not hands-on product testing

Disclosure: This guide is informational. It does not claim personal testing. Product features, prices and availability vary; verify current details with the manufacturer or seller.

Start with the problem you need to detect

Water-leak devices generally fall into two useful groups: point sensors that detect water where they are placed, and systems that monitor a broader plumbing area or water flow. A point sensor can be useful beneath a sink, near a washing machine or beside a water heater. A whole-home system may require compatible plumbing hardware and professional installation.

Check the alert path

A detector is only useful if an alert reaches someone who can act. Check whether it uses a local siren, Wi-Fi, a hub, app notifications or a combination. Confirm whether alerts can still work if the internet connection or power fails. Read the app's notification and account requirements before buying.

Understand placement and maintenance

Think about likely leak locations and whether the sensor can sit flat on the floor. Check battery type, expected battery life as stated by the manufacturer, low-battery alerts, water resistance and how the device is tested after installation.

Questions to ask before purchase

Does the product need a separate hub? Does it work with your Wi-Fi band and phone? Can multiple sensors be managed in one app? Is there a subscription? Does it only detect water at the sensor, or can it shut off water automatically? What happens during a power or internet outage?

Bottom line

Choose based on the leak locations you want to monitor, the reliability of the alert method and the maintenance you are willing to perform. Do not assume a small leak sensor can replace plumbing inspection or an automatic shut-off system.
