Smart Irrigation Controllers: How to Choose the Right Number of Zones

Understand zones, Wi-Fi, weather features, outdoor installation and compatibility before replacing a garden watering timer.

Research guide · Researched 4 November 2026 · Desk research; no hands-on testing claimed

Transparency: This is an editorial buying guide based on publicly available manufacturer documentation and other cited sources. We have not physically tested these products. Product availability, regional compatibility, prices and warranty terms can change. Confirm details for your country before buying.

Start with the irrigation system you already have

A smart irrigation controller does not create a good watering layout by itself. It schedules valves that operate groups of sprinklers or drip lines. Each independently controlled group is commonly called a zone or station. A lawn, vegetable bed, shrubs and container plants may need different schedules, so the number of zones you actually use is a more useful starting point than the number of features in an app.

Two controller families with published specifications

Rachio 3

Rachio lists 4-, 6-, 8-, 12- and 16-zone versions. The company says the controller works with most in-ground irrigation systems, requires a strong Wi-Fi signal at the installation location and uses a smartphone app to connect to Wi-Fi. Outdoor installation requires a separately purchased weatherproof enclosure. Rachio's technical support page also documents its power requirements, supported wire ranges and compatibility notes for valves and sensors.

Sources: Rachio 3 product page · Rachio controller technical specifications.

Orbit B-hyve

Orbit's B-hyve range includes different controller types, including in-ground sprinkler controllers and hose timers. Do not assume that instructions for a hose timer apply to an in-ground multi-zone controller. Orbit's support documentation explains that zones are groups of sprinklers connected to a valve and that the controller runs zones in sequence to maintain suitable water pressure. Its programming guide describes weekday, even/odd-day and interval scheduling options for the documented timer.

Sources: Orbit B-hyve programming guide · Orbit explanation of zones and programs.

How many zones do you need?

  • One hose or tap: a smart hose timer may be enough. Check whether it supports the connector and water-pressure range in your setup.
  • Several garden areas: choose a controller with enough independent zones for the valves already installed, plus a little room for a planned expansion if worthwhile.
  • Large lawn or complex landscaping: count the actual valves and confirm compatibility with master valves, pump-start relays and rain or soil sensors.

Weather intelligence is not a substitute for checking the garden

Some controllers can adjust schedules using weather data or rain-skip features. These functions depend on the product, location, app, internet access and supported integrations. They cannot identify every local condition: shade, soil type, slope, plant maturity, clogged emitters and uneven sprinkler coverage still matter. Observe the garden and adjust schedules rather than assuming an automatic setting is always correct.

Compatibility checklist

  1. Count the existing irrigation valves and zones.
  2. Check the controller's supported voltage and valve type.
  3. Confirm whether your system uses a pump-start relay or master valve.
  4. Check whether a rain sensor, soil sensor or flow sensor is supported.
  5. Measure Wi-Fi signal at the intended controller location.
  6. Confirm whether the unit is indoor-only or needs a separate outdoor enclosure.
  7. Check local water restrictions and seasonal watering rules.

Common buying mistakes

Buying a 16-zone controller for a two-zone garden may add cost without practical benefit. Choosing a hose timer for a wired in-ground sprinkler system may be the wrong product category. Another common mistake is overlooking the power supply or outdoor enclosure. Rachio's documentation explicitly states that outdoor installations need an enclosure and that its controller requires its supplied AC adapter; confirm the current model's local requirements.

Our practical takeaway

Choose by existing valves, water layout and installation environment first. Rachio 3 is a documented option for multi-zone in-ground systems, while B-hyve products cover different controller formats. Compare the exact model rather than the family name, and verify local availability, voltage, plumbing fittings, app support and warranty for your country.

How to use this guide

Use the criteria above to create a shortlist for your own setup. Before purchasing, confirm the exact model number, regional version, current price, compatibility, warranty, return policy and any required accessories with the manufacturer or seller.
